Overview: How I Conducted the Analysis

This document provides a step-by-step account of how I moved from nine interview transcripts to the seven final themes presented in Chapter 4. It is organised around the six phases of Braun and Clarke's (2006) reflexive thematic analysis, adapted to my abductive analytic logic.

Objective
"I want to walk you through exactly how I did the analysis (the process), showing the actual decisions made at each stage, the codes generated from individual participants, how I consolidated them, and how I arrived at the seven themes.
1
Familiarisation — reading all 9 transcripts before coding
I read each transcript twice (during transcription and after) in full before assigning a single code, noting what struck/surprised me (only focusing on the research-relevant info), and where my own experience as a Kenyan educator threatened to shape my reading before the data could.
2
Initial coding — 47 open codes from raw participant extracts
I went line by line through each transcript and assigned initial codes. These were close to the participants' own language. I did not yet think about themes. I documented every coding decision in the spreadsheet below.
3
Focused coding — consolidating 47 codes into 22
After completing initial coding across all 9 transcripts, I looked across the codes and identified clusters. Where codes were doing the same analytical work, I merged them. Where codes required latent-level interpretation rather than surface description, I flagged them. This produced Codebook v2.
4
Theme construction — 7 candidate themes from 22 focused codes
I mapped focused codes onto candidate themes, wrote analytic memos for each theme explaining the interpretive logic, and checked each theme against the full dataset for confirming and disconfirming evidence.
5
Theme review and refinement — checking against data and RQs
I reviewed each theme against every transcript to ensure it held. Where evidence was thin or a code did not fit neatly, I documented the decision. I then checked each theme was addressable by the interview data and aligned to at least one RQ.
6
Member checking — 4 participants confirmed theme summaries
I sent plain-language summaries of all 7 themes to T01, T03, T07, and T09 via WhatsApp. All four responded, rated overall accuracy at 6–7 out of 7, and added three substantive qualifications that I incorporated into the final analysis.
Phase 1: Familiarisation — Reflexive Journal Entries

Before coding, I read all nine transcripts in full twice. The following journal entries document what I noticed, what surprised me, and what analytical risks I identified in my own reading at this stage.

What I will say
"I deliberately did not code anything until I had read the whole corpus. This is Braun and Clarke's requirement for reflexive TA — you have to know what you are dealing with before you start assigning meaning. These are the actual notes I made during familiarisation."
Familiarisation Note — After reading T01 and T08
What struck me immediately: both T01 (22 years, urban) and T08 (3 years public, rural) described arriving early without pay or buying materials from their own pocket. This is not an individual story — it is a structural pattern. I noticed I was drawn to this because it confirms something I suspected from my own knowledge of the Kenyan system. I flagged this as a potential analytical bias risk: I need to make sure I am reading the data, not confirming what I already believe. Decision: I will code this as "personal financial investment" at the semantic level, and then return to ask whether there is a latent dimension that the semantic code does not capture.
Familiarisation Note — After reading T03
T03's account of the boy who cannot copy from the board is the most analytically dense transcript in the corpus. Three years of unresolved effort without any institutional support. What is striking is not the complaint — it is the flatness of "I don't know what to do." This is a teacher who has reached the limit of their adaptive capacity without any system response. I am also noticing that T03 spontaneously describes what the child can do (drawing, colouring, memorising) before I ask about strengths. This is analytically significant and I need a code for it that is separate from the adaptation strategies. It is a framing move, not a strategy.
Familiarisation Note — After reading T04 and T07
T04 is different from the others. The SNE unit context produces a qualitatively different kind of account — there is an IEP, there are gadgets from KISE, there is a pre-vocational programme. But the most striking thing is that T04 is describing a pipeline that is being blocked at the head teacher and parent level. The specialist provision exists; the learners who need it are not being referred. I need to be careful not to let T04's data pull the adaptation theme toward specialist practice. The comparison value is the highest in this transcript. T07 has a co-teacher — the only one described as structurally enabling rather than coincidental.

T03 "There is another principle that we use here in school: I do, we do, then you do. Yeah, it works." scaffolding_gradual_release Semantic Explicit naming of a scaffolding framework. Teacher has not been told about this as a pedagogical strategy — they have named it independently. Connected to Vygotsky's ZPD without knowing Vygotsky.
T04 "When teaching learners with special needs, it comes from your heart. Kama huna roho ya utu, ata kama ume-train on special needs, you cannot teach." vocation_moral_not_professional Latent Latent code: training is necessary but insufficient. The necessary condition named by T04 is moral orientation, not qualification. This is the clearest articulation of "ni moyo" in the corpus and anchors the moral vocation theme.
T04 "The biggest problem comes from the head of institutions who are not ready to assist. But now I have a new head of institution who is very cooperative. Now we are very much okay." leadership_as_enabler_or_barrier Semantic The contrast between two head teachers in the same account is the analytical point. A single leadership change produced a qualitative shift in what T04 could do. This codes at the mesosystem level in ecological terms.
T04 "One time last year, we made a chicken coop with the learner. I bought a hammer, a saw, msumeno. We got materials from broken desks." personal_material_investment_vocational Semantic T04 purchased tools from personal funds for a vocational lesson. This is simultaneously a semantic code (the behaviour) and a latent signifier of the moral vocation — the adaptation required financial sacrifice beyond any professional expectation.
T04 "Some parents, they don't accept that my child has special needs. When I tried to talk with the headteacher, he said, Leave these children, let them stay." referral_blocked_by_parent_and_HT Semantic Two-level barrier: parent denial and leadership dismissal. T04's unit is adjacent to the mainstream school — the barrier is not that there is no specialist provision; it is that the pipeline to that provision is blocked. Critical for accurate reading of T04's positionality.
T05 "A student who reported in Grade 1 is now in Grade 10. That tells you we are almost 10 years. And we are still retooling." retooling_failure_decade_long Semantic The ten-year timeline is the analytical point. This is not a recent gap — it is a structural failure that has persisted across the entire implementation period. One of the most damning quantifications in the corpus.
T05 "To be honest, we were teaching the learners to pass the exam. And since we have been doing it for a while, we are still doing it." exam_logic_persisting_under_CBE Latent Pedagogical inertia as a latent phenomenon: the old system has colonised the new one. "We are not supposed to" (implied) shows awareness of the contradiction. This is not ignorance; it is systemic drift that the teacher recognises but cannot individually arrest.
T05 "Schemes of work are just done because we are being followed. If nobody's coming to check them, we don't do that." formal_structures_compliance_only Latent Critical latent code for the cooperation vs collaboration distinction. Formal structures are performed for inspection, not used for professional development. The contrast with WhatsApp groups in the same interview makes the distinction analytically clear.
T06 "Even when I explain to him there is no school today, he insists on going. I think it is because of the teacher." belonging_as_presence_driver Latent Parent testimony relayed by teacher. The affective pull toward the classroom is not about curriculum — it is about the teacher-learner relationship. Latent code: this is belonging operationalised as a presence indicator. One of the strongest pieces of evidence for the P1 dimension.
T06 "They will not get the concept, but they will love the lesson. You will find that in class, they are very attentive. They are willing, ni vile hawashiki." participation_without_comprehension Semantic T06 is making a sophisticated implicit distinction between participation (P2) and cognitive acquisition (not yet P3). Participation here does not require comprehension — it requires willingness and emotional safety. Key evidence for P2 operationalisation.
T07 "We always have another teacher in the room. The co-teacher will make sure that the learner who is differently abled will get into the group." co_teaching_structured_differentiation Semantic Co-teaching enables real-time differentiated support without extracting the learner from the group. Distinct from all other collaborative practices in the sample — this is genuine collaboration, not cooperation.
T07 "When she was in Grade 1, she could not communicate right, but right now you can hear her calling teacher. She can come tell you, Huyu anapiga." progression_longitudinal_communication Semantic Two-year progression arc documented by the same teacher. This is the clearest example of P3 (progression) measured against individual baseline rather than curriculum norm. The milestone (initiating verbal communication) is not in the CBE framework. T07 constructed it from observation.
T08 "It is costly, but now you have to do it. You just do it. You just close your eyes and do it." resilience_moral_compulsion_not_calculation Latent "Close your eyes and do it" is phenomenologically precise: the teacher does not weigh cost and decide it is worth it. They suspend the calculation. This is the latent logic of moral vocation expressed in its most direct form. Strongest anchor for the T5 theme.
T08 "Monanda had taken the sports pathway; it does not have a field, no teachers. The school has 13 learners." pathway_empty_designation Semantic Specific empirical instance of the pathway-rhetoric-reality gap. The pathway exists on paper and is non-functional on the ground. T08's rural context makes this the most visible exposure of the CBE paradox in the corpus.
T08 "Attendance changes. In January we have very few pupils. When we approach the planting season, they often come back to school to escape the farm labour." presence_seasonal_poverty_driven Semantic Rural poverty drives attendance patterns directly. Farm labour cycle as an external macrosystem constraint on P1. Counterpoint to urban near-universal attendance — shows context-dependence of the first P.
T09 "Instead of him writing because he might take the whole day, he can answer orally. If it's something to do with drawing and writing, we can do drawing and reading. I can also make some matching." oral_alternatives_multimodal_expression Semantic Three alternative response modalities offered without prompting in a single sentence. This is the phenomenology of adaptive expertise — rapid generation of options under uncertainty. Key evidence for UDL expression cluster.
T09 "My co-teacher is an SNE teacher. It was just a coincidence that she's an SNE." co_teaching_incidental_not_designed Latent "Coincidence" is the analytically important word. The most enabling collaborative arrangement in the sample was not designed — it arose by chance. Latent implication: the system does not produce this by design. Strongest evidence for the ecological argument about incidental collaboration.
T09 "When they are accepted by others, they try. But if they feel that they are neglected, they withdraw." belonging_enables_participation Latent T09 is theorising the causal chain: belonging → engagement → exposure → potential acquisition. This is Vygotsky's social mediation logic without Vygotsky. Latent code for the P2 dimension of inclusive utility.

Phase 4: Theme Construction and Analytic Memos

From 22 focused codes, I constructed 7 candidate themes. For each theme, I wrote an analytic memo documenting the interpretive logic, the central analytical claim, and any counter-evidence I checked for. Selected memos are shown below.

What I will say
"This is where interpretation rather than description begins. Each theme required me to ask not just what the codes have in common, but what claim can be made about them — what they reveal about teachers' practice, reasoning, or context that was not visible in any single extract."
Analytic Memo — Theme 5: Moral Vocation
The decision to make this a standalone theme rather than absorbing it into Theme 1 (Adaptation Ensembles) was the hardest of the theme construction phase. The argument for absorption: moral vocation is what produces the adaptation ensembles, so it belongs as a substrate of Theme 1. The argument for separation: moral vocation explains the sustainability of adaptive effort, not the form it takes. It speaks directly to the "pedagogical reasoning" component of RQ1, whereas Theme 1 speaks to the "what teachers do" component. Decision: keep separate. The tension between them is itself analytically productive — the themes illuminate each other by being distinct. Counter-evidence check: are there participants whose adaptive behaviour is not accompanied by any evidence of moral orientation? No — all nine described some form of personal investment. The latent code "vocation_heart" is present at some level in every transcript.
Analytic Memo — Theme 4: Lateral Collaboration (counter-evidence check)
T05 is the strongest counter-evidence to any claim that collaboration is functional. "Schemes of work are just done because we are being followed" directly disconfirms the idea that formal structures support genuine professional exchange. But T05 also participates in WhatsApp sharing — so the counter-evidence is not against the theme, it is against any simplistic version of it. The resolution: Theme 4 holds if it is framed as "lateral cooperation compensates for absent formal collaboration" rather than "teachers collaborate." The distinction between cooperation and collaboration is not imposed by me — it is what the data shows when you read T05's formal and informal descriptions side by side. The Harris (2013) framework is a sensitising lens that names the distinction, not a framework imposed on the data.
Analytic Memo — Deciding the sequence of themes in Chapter 4
The seven themes are not presented in the order they emerged in the data or in numerical order. I made a deliberate sequencing decision: begin with Theme 6 (Neurodivergence Recognition) because it establishes the conditions — who the teachers are trying to include — before moving to what they do (Theme 1), how they judge it (Theme 2), why they persist (Theme 5), and then outward to the collaborative and systemic conditions (Themes 4, 3, 7). This sequence builds an argument rather than listing findings. The analytical logic is: establish the learner profile, then trace the teacher response, then contextualise it ecologically.

Closing statement for the meeting
"The process I have just walked you through is the one that produced the analysis in Chapter 4. Every code has a paper trail: it started as an extract from a specific participant, was assigned an initial code with a rationale, was reviewed in the consolidation pass, was mapped to a theme with an analytic memo, was checked against counter-evidence, and for the four participants who responded to member checking, was confirmed as resonant with their experience. I am not claiming the analysis is objective — reflexive thematic analysis does not make that claim. But it is accountable: every interpretive decision can be traced back to a documented moment in this process."
